When the doors close at the end of the day, your body is drained, the shop floor felt fully occupied, but your bank balance at the end of the month doesn't reflect all that effort.

Why does this happen in so many auto repair shops?

The answer is simple: **a lack of narrative clarity and lift-by-lift shop floor control**. Most shops only track vehicle intake and the final bill, ignoring what actually happens during the hours a car sits on a lift.

To fix this, **CoreAutoCRM** introduced the **3-Act Operational Logbook** concept—a daily intelligence framework that analyzes the real-time movement of Repair Orders (ROs) and generates a brutally honest executive summary for management.

---

## What Is the 3-Act Operational Logbook?

The Logbook abandons static reports filled with confusing spreadsheets. Instead, it divides the shop day into three logical governance phases:

![3-Act Operational Logbook](/images/posts/diario-de-bordo-3-atos-infografico.png)

---

## ACT 1: What Was Supposed to Happen (Morning Intention)

The first act defines the clear intention of the day based on the vehicles allocated to lifts and bays.

In a shop tracked by the system, morning planning maps out potential revenue from pending estimates and assigns technicians by bay:
* **Lift 1:** Brake and ignition inspection on the Renault Kwid with technician Gabriel.
* **Lift 2:** Suspension, rotor, and pad analysis on the Chevrolet Montana with technician Jefferson.
* **Alignment Bay:** Transmission and CV joint check on the Volkswagen Voyage with technician Natanael.

When the shop owner starts the day knowing that pending estimates represent **$6,051.00 in potential revenue**, the front desk and parts counter have a clear goal to pursue.

---

## ACT 2: What Actually Happened (The Shop Floor Battle)

In the second act, the system tracks real physical movement throughout the day—recording completed services, confirmed revenue, and technical challenges faced by technicians.

If technicians spend all day focused on teardowns, OBD2 scanner testing, and inspection, diagnoses are completed with precision. However, the report highlights a critical insight: **if diagnoses are complete but no RO has moved to the execution phase, confirmed revenue for the day remains zero on paper**.

This clarity prevents shop owners from fooling themselves into thinking the shop was profitable just because the bay was noisy and busy all day.

---

## ACT 3: Lessons Learned & Legacy (Brutally Honest Management)

The third act is where AI governance delivers the executive feedback shop owners need to hear—without sugarcoating:

> *"Having three vehicles occupying lifts all day solely for diagnostic work, without rapid estimate approval from customers to begin repair work, directly harms your shop floor turnover. A lift stuck on diagnosis longer than necessary is an underutilized revenue engine."*

The lessons learned outline immediate tactical actions for the next morning:
1. **Accelerate Estimate Follow-ups:** The front desk must prioritize contacting customers whose vehicles are occupying lifts.
2. **Parts Department Alignment:** Ensure brake kits, suspension parts, and ignition components are quoted and ready for order the moment customer approval is granted.
3. **Free Up Hydraulic Lifts:** Convert stuck estimate potential into real revenue and clear bays for the next incoming queue.

---

## Reliable Revenue Calculation via Item Matrix

Another key innovation is eliminating cash flow discrepancies. Instead of relying on a single manually entered total, the platform calculates accumulated revenue directly from the estimate item matrix (`orcamento_json.itens`), clearly separating **labor** (high margin) from **parts** (lower margin).

Viewing the daily closing with total revenue (such as **$21,405.90 across 17 Repair Orders**), the shop owner knows exactly where every dollar of margin came from.

---

## Conclusion: Stop Operating in the Dark

Working hard without seeing profit isn't a lack of technical skill; it's a **lack of daily governance over lift turnover**.

By adopting the 3-Act Logbook, your shop gains operational discipline:
* **Morning:** Align goals and assign technicians to bays.
* **Afternoon:** Track diagnostic progress with proper tools and scanners.
* **Evening:** Evaluate lessons learned and make immediate decisions on pending estimates.
